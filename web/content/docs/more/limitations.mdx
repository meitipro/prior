## What it does not do

- **It proves who sealed first, not who wrote it,** and it gives no legal rights.
- **A revealed text is public.** Seal a description, not a secret.
- **Overlap is a judgment, and a model can be wrong.** In the evaluation the validators answered PARTIAL where the spec expected NONE on two short, generic later texts. Those misses are published, and the prompt was not changed to fit them.
- **A broad entry earns PARTIAL at most.** The prompt ignores what is common in the field.
- **Test GEN has no value.** The money mechanics are real; the money on Studio Next is not.
- **Seals are for text only.** Seals cannot carry a file or an image, and the judge compares two texts of at most 1,500 characters each.

## Where this build differs from the spec

1. **The runtime header.** The spec names `py-genlayer:5jycge4q…`. Studio Next now rejects that hash (`invalid_contract runner malformed`), so the contract is pinned to the runner the network itself resolves `latest` to today. See [Addresses](/docs/reference/addresses).
2. **A 48 hour bound on reading.** A challenge nobody could read is voided with its stake returned, and the pair can be filed again, so no money waits forever. This adds a fifth outcome, VOID, that is not one of the four verdicts.
3. **An exact text.** The seal is over the text exactly as written. A reveal that differs by one space does not match.
4. **Stretch goals are not built:** entry versions, group entries, embeds and format registries.
